    Hardware

    Every node lists its own hardware from /sys and keeps one Device per piece in the store, applied when it turns up and deleted when it is gone (a rescan every half minute): kith get device lists them. A Device is named NODE-CLASS-NAME (lower case, cut to 63 characters); its spec has node, class and the device node as path. The class is gpu (a DRM card, /dev/dri/card0), display (a connector with a screen; no path), input (/dev/input/event3), audio (a sound card, /dev/snd/controlC0), camera (/dev/video0) or usb (not a hub, /dev/bus/usb/001/004).

    • Labels (a selector can name them). All: kith.dev/node, class, kith.dev/discovered: "true". GPUs and USB devices also vendor, model; a GPU driver and, where known, vram in bytes, and dmem, the region of its memory where its driver gives the kernel's dmem cgroup one and kithd can give its containers the controller (drm/0000:03:00.0/vram); inputs and sound cards model. An input also identity, a hash of its name, port, serial and ids: another device behind the same event index is taken from its holder. A USB device also bus, its port (1-2), and serial, a hash of its serial where it says one: its ids are the stick's own to say, so name one stick by serial or bus with kith.dev/node; another serial (or none) in its port takes it from its holder.
    • NODE-seat0 is a Seat naming the node's displays, inputs, sound cards and GPUs. What carries kith.dev/discovered is kept in step (rewritten if you edit it, deleted when the hardware goes); a Device or Seat you wrote yourself is never touched.
    • Do not name nodes alpha and alpha-gpu: a Device belongs to the node with the longest name that starts it. A join that would take a listed Device is refused.
    • NVIDIA's own driver. A card on it also has nodes: what CUDA, OpenGL and Vulkan open beside the card (/dev/nvidiactl, nvidia-uvm, nvidia-uvm-tools, nvidia-modeset, /dev/nvidiaN), those that exist. Not tried on a real card.

    Claiming hardware #

    A workload names DeviceClaims (same namespace) in its template's deviceClaims. A claim names a seat, a device or a selector over Device labels, and access: exclusive (default) or shared:

    apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: DeviceClaim
metadata: { name: render }
spec:
  selector: { class: gpu, kith.dev/node: n1 }
  access: exclusive
---
apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: Workload
metadata: { name: renderer }
spec:
  template:
    containers: [{ name: run, image: example/renderer }]
    deviceClaims: [render]
    • The workload runs only on the node of the Devices its claims reach. A selector that matches Devices on several nodes takes those of the lowest-named node; add kith.dev/node to fix the node. Claims that reach two nodes hold nothing (the status says so).
    • One workload holds a Device at a time. A second exclusive holder waits (unschedulable: waiting for DeviceClaim render, held by Workload/default/renderer) and takes over when the first is gone. shared claims may have any number of holders, none beside an exclusive one. kith get deviceclaim render -o yaml shows status.node, devices, holders or message.
    • A container gets its Devices' nodes in its own /dev, beside null, zero, full, random, urandom, tty; a GPU brings its render node (/dev/dri/renderD*). A missing Device or claim stops the unit and says which. /dev/mem, /dev/kmem, /dev/port and /dev/tpm* are given to no pod, whatever a Device says.
    • On kith OS a device filter on each container's cgroup checks every open and mknod: it lets through the usual few, the terminals of the container's own /dev/pts and each device it was given, and refuses the rest, even to a root pod. A pod that made its own node (/dev/net/tun), or a FUSE program (/dev/fuse; kith OS's kernel has FUSE), needs a Device with class: other and its path, claimed. Where kithd is your own service there is no filter: no one in a user namespace can make a node.
    • The right to apply a DeviceClaim and a workload that names it is the grant: a Role rule with kinds: ["*"] covers both, so give it only to someone you trust with any hardware.

    Who may claim what. A Device or Seat is given to namespaces by its label kith.dev/namespaces, a comma-separated list (default, desktop). A claim in namespace N reaches it only if the label lists N, or N is kith-system, the cluster's own. With no label, a gpu is open to every namespace, and every other Device (input, audio, camera, display, usb, other) and every Seat is kith-system's alone. A seat claim also needs each of the Seat's labelled Devices to be given to N. The label is an admin's (metadata.labels, kith apply -f): discovery keeps it, and a worker cannot add, change or drop it. Removing it refuses a claim at the next pass; its status.message says why. A labelled Device or Seat whose hardware is gone is kept, so the label is there when it comes back, and marked kith.dev/absent: "true" until then: a claim naming it is refused ("is absent") and a selector passes over it.

    A share of a GPU's vram. A shared claim may say share: { vram: 512Mi }: what it asks of each Device it holds, counted against the Device's vram label. A workload's shares add up; one that does not fit waits, and its message gives the numbers (status.share). On a Device with a dmem label the share is also a ceiling: each of the workload's containers gets the sum of its shares there as dmem.max in its cgroup, and the kernel refuses it more of the GPU's memory (status.share.DEVICE.enforced: true). That is where the driver gives the region (amdgpu, xe, NVIDIA's) and kithd may hand the controller down to its containers (root, or a delegation with dmem; a kithd that cannot gives its Devices no dmem label). Elsewhere a share is cooperative. Read back on a PC with a Radeon iGPU and kithd as root, a 256Mi share gave the container's cgroup drm/0000:74:00.0/vram 268435456 in dmem.max, and the claim enforced: true. Untried: MPS, MIG partitions.

    An app as a workload of its own, in a session's window #

    A DeviceClaim on a seat with access: client takes none of the seat's devices, so any number of workloads hold it beside the session, each on the seat's node, in the session's namespace and only while a workload of that namespace holds the seat. Each gets the render nodes of the seat's GPUs (not the card) and the seat's directory /run/seat, to read. A session that puts its Wayland and PipeWire sockets there (the desktop packages set XDG_RUNTIME_DIR=/run/seat) is where the app opens its window:

    apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: DeviceClaim
metadata: { name: office-apps }
spec: { seat: office-pc-seat0, access: client }
---
apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: Workload
metadata: { name: editor }
spec:
  template:
    deviceClaims: [office-apps]
    containers:
      - name: editor
        image: registry.example/me/editor:3
        env:
          - { name: WAYLAND_DISPLAY, value: /run/seat/wayland-0 }
          - { name: PIPEWIRE_RUNTIME_DIR, value: /run/seat }
    • The app has its own image, limits, volumes and rollback, and shows as one of the claim's status.holders. It must run as the session's user: the directory is that user's alone.
    • Input hot-plug. An input device plugged in or pulled while a container holds its seat appears in or leaves its /dev/input at the next scan, with no restart (on kith OS, for a container that had an input device at start); elsewhere, or when a changed claim or seat takes one away, the unit restarts. Not yet: an app on another node shown in this session; a NodeWorkload claim. A client of a session is in it: see SECURITY.md.

    Running a Flatpak app in a session #

    examples/session/ is a session and one Flatpak app in its window, both in the desktop namespace on the seat desk-seat0. After the seat exists, kith apply -f examples/session/session.yaml applies it; put your own image names and app ID in place of the placeholders.

    What makes it work:

    • One user. The session and the app both run as uid 1000 (runAsUser), a user the example's Containerfile makes. The seat's directory is that user's alone, so an app as another user cannot open the sockets in it.
    • The session holds the seat. desk-seat is exclusive, so the session takes the seat's devices. session-start.sh starts the session bus at /run/seat/bus and the compositor on /run/seat/wayland-0, with XDG_RUNTIME_DIR=/run/seat.
    • The app is a client. desk-apps has access: client: it takes no seat device, only the seat's directory and the render nodes. Its WAYLAND_DISPLAY and DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS point at those sockets. Its XDG_RUNTIME_DIR is its own, /run/user/1000, because the seat's directory is read-only to it.
    • A home that lasts. HOME is /home/app, a Volume (app-home) on the seat's node, so the app's data outlives its container. userNamespaces: true lets Flatpak's sandbox start, and readOnlyRoot: false lets it install into the image's root.

    Untried: nothing here has run on a seat. Not checked: that Flatpak accepts an absolute WAYLAND_DISPLAY, that weston reaches the DRM device as uid 1000 without the video and render groups, and that flatpak run finds an app installed in the image. The repository does not build or pull the example image. An app that shares the session's user, bus and seat directory is in the session's trust: see SECURITY.md.

    What a seat keeps for itself #

    apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: Seat
metadata: { name: desk-seat0 }
spec:
  node: desk
  devices: [desk-gpu-card0]
  reserve: { cpu: 4, memory: 8Gi }     # kept for whatever holds the seat
---
apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: Workload
metadata: { name: training }
spec:
  template:
    containers: [{ name: run, image: registry.example/train:1 }]
    yieldTo: { seat: desk-seat0, when: [fullscreen] }   # step back while a game is up
    • A seat's reserve comes off its node for every workload that does not hold the seat, held or not. The holder fits only what it wants beyond it; its units are guaranteed the reserve's memory (memory.min) and go first for CPU and disk (cpu.weight and io.weight at 10000; all else 100). CPUs are not pinned; a GPU is not shared out (not yet).
    • A session says what its seat is doing with POST /v1/seats/desk-seat0/activity?state=active|idle|fullscreen (it needs an Identity whose Role may apply Seats); it shows as status.activity. A seat nobody holds is idle.
    • A workload with yieldTo runs on a twentieth of a CPU on that seat's node while the seat is in a state it names, and gets its limit back when the state changes (about ten seconds).
    • A template that says priority: idle goes further down: cpu.idle, the lowest io.weight and a memory.high, on every node and whatever the seat does. Batch work beside a desktop takes only spare cycles (see idle work).

    Virtual machines #

    A template's vms are virtual machines, beside or instead of its containers and exec units, in a Workload, a Job or anything with a template:

    apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: Workload
metadata: { name: windows }
spec:
  placement: { require: { node.labels.seat: desk } }
  template:
    deviceClaims: [card]              # a DeviceClaim for a GPU set aside for machines
    vms:
      - name: machine
        firmware: /usr/share/edk2/x64/OVMF.4m.fd
        disks: [{ path: /var/lib/machines/windows.raw }]
        cpus: 8
        memory: 16Gi
        resources: { cpu: 8, memory: 17Gi }   # what it costs the node; else its cpus and memory
    • A machine starts from a kernel (a microVM; initrd, cmdline) or from its disks through firmware. memory is required; cpus is 1 unless said. A disk is a raw image (readOnly: true if it must not be written): a path is a file on the node.
    • A disk in a replicated Volume. disks: [{ volume: home, file: disk.img, size: 40Gi }] names a Volume instead of a path (one of the two, not both; file is disk.img unless said, one name in the Volume's directory). The machine runs where the Volume is, as a container that mounts it does, and kithd makes the image there once, sparse and size big, if the file is missing; it never resizes or overwrites one that exists. A node without the Volume is not given the machine: it says why in the unit's note. When the Volume is handed over to another replica, the machine follows it and starts from the same image. Keep one replica of the machine, and mount the Volume nowhere else: a container that shares its directory could swap the image for a link between kithd's check and the hypervisor's open.
    • kithd supervises the hypervisor; the serial console (console=ttyS0) is the unit's log: kith logs Workload/windows --unit machine. hypervisor: qemu (default, with KVM) or cloudHypervisor; the node needs qemu-system-x86_64 (-aarch64 on arm64) or cloud-hypervisor on its PATH, and /dev/kvm.
    • Passing a device through (VFIO). A PCI device bound to vfio-pci is a Device of class pci (desk-pci-0000-01-00.0; labels driver: vfio-pci, vendor, model and function: gpu, audio, usb or other). It is not in the node's seat and class: gpu never selects it: claim it by name, or by class: pci and its function. A vms unit gets every such Device its template claims; a container never does. The owner binds it before kithd starts (vfio-pci.ids=10de:2684,10de:22ba, IOMMU on).
    • Not yet: Firecracker; a network beyond QEMU's own NAT (the machine reaches out, nothing reaches it); live migration of a machine with a Volume disk; kithd binding vfio-pci; vms on kith OS. Tested: a Job boots a kernel under KVM. Not tested: cloud-hypervisor, a real GPU passed through.

    Switching one GPU #

    A desktop and a machine can take turns on one GPU of a node, with no reboot. It needs an IOMMU on, vfio-pci in the node's kernel (on kith OS the vfio spec prepares the kernel with both; the rest, vms on kith OS, is not there yet) and the card bound to vfio-pci by its owner (kithd never binds or unbinds a driver). The session and the machine each name one exclusive DeviceClaim for it:

    kith switch gpu windows   # the machine takes the card
kith switch gpu desktop   # the session takes it back
    • A claim's holder (exclusive claims only) is the workload that takes the Device ahead of others, from a holder of its own namespace; a holder of another keeps it and the named workload waits. The current holder stops, then the new one starts, and stays until another switch. A card keeps its pci label under either driver: selector: { pci: "0000:01:00.0" }.
    • What a switch loses. It takes seconds: the session stops, its windows are lost and the desktop comes back as a new session. An app that is its own workload waits for a session to hold the seat again; its Volumes are kept.
    • Not built: two GPUs at once, a frame relay. Proven only with stand-ins: no real GPU, VFIO bind or driver unbind was tried.

    Confidential machines #

    confidential: true runs a template's machines in memory the node cannot read (SEV-SNP, TDX):

    apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: Workload
metadata: { name: sealed }
spec:
  placement: { require: { node.labels.cpu: epyc } }
  template:
    confidential: true
    vms:
      - name: machine
        firmware: /usr/share/edk2/x64/OVMF.amdsev.fd   # one built for SEV-SNP (or TDX)
        disks: [{ path: /var/lib/machines/sealed.raw }]
        memory: 8Gi
    • A node runs it only if its KVM has SEV-SNP or TDX (kvm_amd/parameters/sev_snp or kvm_intel/parameters/tdx in /sys/module reads Y). A node with neither runs none of it and says why in the status' notes; nothing falls back to memory in the clear. Only vms units with a firmware, under QEMU: any other unit here is refused at apply.
    • Not yet: the scheduler knows nothing of which nodes have such a processor (use placement); no attestation; containers inside such a machine; cloud-hypervisor. Not tested: a guest on SEV-SNP or TDX hardware, only QEMU's options and the refusal elsewhere.

    Tried on a real PC #

    kith OS 1.0.0 on an AMD mini PC (a Ryzen with a Radeon 780M), booted over HTTP from the firmware, claimed with --claim, and a desktop Linux PC beside it on the same LAN:

    • Ran: images pulled from Docker Hub; two replicas of a web server, ready by an HTTP probe, one port with publish: true reached from the other PC; pod to pod over IPv4 and IPv6.
    • A desktop on the real screen: a Workload in kith-system claiming the seat (NODE-seat0: the card and two inputs) runs Alpine's weston (DRM backend, pixman renderer) at 2560x1440 with a terminal. seatd in a container cannot make the seat active (there is no VT), so the session runs with LIBSEAT_BACKEND=noop, as root in its container.
    • The other PC as a worker: its kithd (not root, a user's process) joined with a worker token; Jobs placed on it with placement: { require: { kith.dev/trust: worker } } ran an exec unit and a plain container, their logs read through the first node; kith delete node/NAME then took it out. A pod: true template does not start on a kithd that is not root ("failed to prepare rootfs").
    • Not working yet: the GPU has only the firmware's framebuffer (no amdgpu in the plain image; the gpu-amd-phoenix spec builds it in, not yet run here). (A Service used to have no status and its name reached nothing on a node started without --mesh, which kith OS's default is: now its address is on the node's loopback there, and a pod on that node reaches it, T-0723.)
