    People's devices into the home network: peers, levels and where a level holds

    Status: design, before any code (T-0322, step 1). Nothing here is built. It builds on the Exit kind, its tunnel and its kernel options as they stand on T-0321's branch (not merged): that lands first. A security review reads this record before close; what it finds is decided under "Review".

    The problem #

    The owner wants WireGuard into their home for phones and laptops that are not nodes: "into my network but not necessarily the cluster", with "different levels, like guest, family, admin", usable from a phone with the stock app, "super secure". A level both allows and refuses: a guest reaches one Service, family reaches the LAN and more, admin reaches the cluster's API. The tunnel's DNS goes through a filter the owner runs. And "multiple different wireguard exits": a level's internet may leave by a VPN provider's tunnel.

    What is not wanted: a phone on the mesh, a phone as an Identity, a port open unless a node is told to open it, or anything kith installs on the phone.

    What a peer and a level are #

    Two cluster-scoped kinds, declared with the kinds! list (crates/kith-api/src/lib.rs) and spec!, so unknown fields are refused (deny_unknown_fields, kinds.rs), and checked by Spec::check. Peer and Device are taken, hence the names.

    apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: AccessLevel
metadata: { name: family }
spec:
  networks: [10.1.0.0/24]        # ranges passed on to, IPv4 or IPv6
  services: [media/films, home/photos]   # namespace/name: each TCP port; "*": every Service
  dns: 10.1.0.53                 # the resolver its peers are given
  exit: nl-1                     # internet by this Exit only, or not at all
---
apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: AccessLevel
metadata: { name: admin }
spec:
  networks: [10.1.0.0/24, 10.9.0.0/16]
  services: ["*"]
  api: true                      # the cluster's API port on the node that ends the tunnel
  dns: 10.1.0.53
  internet: true                 # by the home's own connection
---
apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: AccessLevel
metadata: { name: guest }
spec: { services: [media/films], dns: 10.1.0.53, internet: true }
---
apiVersion: kith.dev/v1
kind: RemotePeer
metadata: { name: phone-1 }
spec:
  node: n1                       # the node that ends its tunnel
  level: family
  publicKey: xTIBA5rboUvnH4htodjb6e697QjLERt1NAB4mZqp8Dg=

    Every field of a level is optional; an empty level reaches nothing. The check refuses: a range that does not parse, or a /0 ("say internet"); a Service not namespace/name or *; dns that is no address; internet and exit together; internet or exit without dns (a phone that sends everything here needs a resolver). A peer's three fields are required, and publicKey is 32 bytes in base64. The node writes a peer's status: its two addresses, seconds since its last handshake (Device::peers, netlink.rs), and why it is not on the device, if not. Not the phone's endpoint: that is where the phone is, and the store keeps history on every node.

    What is stored is the public key only. A private key kith makes is in one answer and nowhere else ("How a configuration is shown once"). A peer may bring its own: the app makes the key, and the owner applies the RemotePeer with its public half.

    Addresses come from the name, as a Service's does (kith_mesh::service_address): one in 100.89.0.0/16 (16 bits of the name's hash; pods have 100.88.0.0/16, pod.rs) and one in a /64 of a ULA /48 made from the cluster's CA with another seed than the mesh's (kith_mesh::prefix), so in_mesh is false for a peer. Two names with one IPv4 address: the second is kept off the device, its status saying "name it otherwise", as two Exits with one number are (exits.rs, wanted). No allocator, nothing to keep.

    A peer cannot claim another's address. Its allowed addresses on the node's device are its own /32 and /128 only, and WireGuard drops a decrypted packet whose source is not among the sending key's, before any rule sees it. Peer.allowed is IPv6 /80s today (lib.rs; netlink.rs, set_peers); it becomes address-and-length pairs, so set_peers serves both devices.

    Only a node its owner tells ends tunnels, by kithd flags as --mesh and --ingress are (main.rs): --people [PORT] (KITH_PEOPLE, 51821 bare; needs --mesh and root) and --people-endpoint NAME:PORT, what a configuration names as the server. No store write opens a port on a node. Without the flag there is no device, table or port, and what an earlier run with it left is taken away at start. With it, the kernel's WireGuard listens on that UDP port on every address. A peer whose node lacks the flag is on no device, and its status says so. The node's key for this device is a file beside the mesh's (pki/people.key, made by mesh.rs's private_key), one per node, never in the store; its public half, port and endpoint go in the Node's status as people, as mesh does.

    How a phone finds it: the endpoint the owner states. The owner forwards that UDP port on their router, or the node has a public address. kith opens no port on a router: the tree has no UPnP, NAT-PMP or PCP code, and this adds none.

    Where a level is enforced #

    On the node that ends the tunnels, in the kernel, in three places of its own.

    A second WireGuard device, kwg0, apart from the mesh's kith0, by the same writer (Device::make and set on T-0321's branch; set_peers, netlink.rs); not kp… or kx…, prefixes kith-pods matches (pod.rs, nat). It holds 100.89.0.1/16 and ::1.

    A table of its own, inet kith-people, by the nfnetlink batch pods use (pod.rs; link_name, verdict, answering, marked on T-0321's branch), rebuilt whole on every change in one all-or-nothing batch, as nat is. Base chains see the node's every packet, so their policy is accept; they judge everything of kwg0, and each one's last rule drops it.

    Chain (hook)MatchesVerdict
    pre (prerouting)source in 100.89.0.0/16 or the people /64, in by any link but kwg0drop
    in (to the node itself)in by kwg0, source a peer's addressjump <level>-in
    fwd (forward)in by kwg0, source a peer'smark the connection as the peer's; jump <level>-out
    fwdout by kwg0, destination a peer'sjump <level>-back
    out (output)out by kwg0, destination a peer'sjump <level>-replies
    in, fwd, outin or out by kwg0, whatever no rule above tookdrop
    post (postrouting, nat)source in the people ranges, out by any link but kwg0masquerade

    A level's chains, one set per level, each ending in drop:

    • -in: a named Service's mesh address with one of its TCP ports (each Service, each port; * all); the API's port, with api (on any of the node's addresses: input is only what is addressed to the node); the resolver's address on 53 and 853, when it is the node's.
    • -out: the resolver's address on UDP and TCP 53 and 853; then any other 53 or 853 dropped; a named range; then private IPv4 (10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16, 100.64/10, 169.254/16) and the networks of the node's own links dropped; then the internet: with internet: true, out by any link but the mesh's, kp* and kx*; with exit, out by that Exit's kx<number> and no other.
    • -back: connection state established or related, and the far end as -out would take it now: a source in a named range; the resolver with source port 53 or 853; for the internet, in by the Exit's kx<number> (an exit) or a public source in by none of the mesh, pod and exit links (home).
    • -replies: established or related, from a named Service's address and port, the API's port, or the resolver; and ICMP errors, so a refused IPv6 fails at once on the phone.

    A range is one masked compare each way (as answering writes one), a Service one rule per port each way (ServiceSpec, service_address). One rule per peer jumps to its level's chains, so a peer moving level changes one rule. A named range that holds the mesh /48, the people ranges or 100.88/16 is left out, and the level's status says which. New expressions beyond pod.rs's: address compares, nfproto/l4proto, source port, jump, !=. No sets.

    Routing rules by exit.rs's Rule (T-0321), which gains the suppressed prefix length as a field (31 for a pod as now, 0 for a peer). exit.rs holds priorities 9000 to 9005, each matching an exit pod's link or a tunnel's own address, so none meets a peer's packet.

    PriorityFamilyMatchesGoes to
    9010bothin by kwg0main without its default routes (the LAN, the mesh, the node)
    9011IPv4in by kwg0, from a peer of a level with an exitthe Exit's table (0x6b78_0000 and its number)
    9011IPv4in by kwg0, from a peer of a level with internet: truemain
    9012bothin by kwg0unreachable

    A narrowed level or a removed peer holds within seconds, open connections too. The loop wakes on every store change (as exits.rs's run, on the generation) and every 10 s. Every packet, both ways, is checked against the level as it is now; connection state only tells a reply from a request, never lets one through alone. Flushed: the connections carrying the peer's mark (0x6b79 and 16 bits of the peer), by exit.rs's forget with the mark as an argument, which ends the masquerade's state too. A removed peer is first taken off the device (RemoveMe), then its rules, then the flush. kithd's Service proxy holds such a connection, passing nothing, until its idle time (an hour, services.rs, IDLE).

    Peers are kept from the mesh (no level chain accepts out by kith0; the node's mesh address is input, where only Service addresses are taken), from each other (none accepts out by kwg0; a range holding the people ranges is left out) and from the node's own ports: -in takes Services, the API port and the resolver; --dns, ingress, NFS, netboot, the mesh's port and ports pods publish (pods.rs, publish, T-0321) are refused.

    Two changes to what is there. kith-pods' guard drops everything forwarded that is not a pod's, nat rebuilds it whole (pod.rs), and an accept in one table overrides no drop in another: so nat takes the people link, as it takes the mesh's on T-0321's branch, and passes it both ways for kith-people to judge. And kithd's Service listener serves only mesh sources (services.rs: accept, and the strays in run); on the node that ends tunnels it also serves the people ranges. A peer's connection then goes on as this node's, and NetworkPolicies see this node (reach asks admits with its address).

    Between start and the rules, nothing passes. Each step only once the last is acknowledged: (1) pod::node, the guard then forwarding; (2) the table with its drops, which match the link's name and so hold before the link exists, as exit.rs's hold relies on; (3) routing rules 9012 and 9010; (4) the device, made or found, its peers emptied first (a restarted kithd finds the kernel's old device, and a peer deleted meanwhile would pass), then key, port, addresses, up; (5) level chains, jumps and rules 9011 in one batch; (6) the peers, from a fresh store (on a worker a fresh view, docs/adr-workers.md). A rule that cannot be written leaves the device without peers, and the Node's status says why. A clean stop takes the device away; after a crash the kernel keeps the last rules.

    DNS #

    A level names its resolver (dns): the address its peers' configuration gives (DNS =), the owner's filter on the LAN or any other address. -out and -in take UDP and TCP 53 and 853 to that address only, and drop 53 and 853 anywhere else, the node's own --dns included. UDP 853 is DNS over QUIC, refused with the rest.

    What that does not stop, said in the README beside the kind: DNS over HTTPS (443, the web's port) and over HTTP/3; a resolver on any other port; anything inside another tunnel. A level with internet lets a peer around the filter by any of these. An app with a resolver hard-coded on 53 is refused and sees no answer.

    To LAN hosts every peer is the node (masquerade), so the filter sees one client (see Open). .kith names are answered by kithd's DNS only: the filter forwards kith. there. kithd's resolver forwards only for private and ULA clients (may_forward, kith-dns/src/server.rs), where 100.64.0.0/10 is not private: by IPv4 a peer gets no name it does not hold.

    Exits #

    A level with exit: nl-1 sends its internet by that Exit or not at all. As built on T-0321's branch, an Exit is node, secret (a wg0.conf in kith-system, read by kithd), namespaces (whose pods may name it) and dns (its pods' resolver); exits.rs holds the tunnel on its node as kx<number>, with a table whose one route is the tunnel (exit.rs, ensure). A level reuses, as built: the tunnel, table and number (exits.rs, tend); Rule for 9011, which takes a /32 source (from) beside iif (hold's per-link rules are not used: a level's peers share one link); kith-pods' rules taking only answers in by kx*; forget, the mark as an argument; and the kernel options T-0321 adds (IP_MULTIPLE_TABLES, IPV6_MULTIPLE_TABLES, NF_CONNTRACK_MARK, NF_CT_NETLINK).

    One change to the kind: namespaces says nothing of levels, so an Exit gains levels, the levels whose peers may leave by it, and its check asks for one of the two. Whoever writes the Exit says who spends its provider's key, for levels as for pods. A level its Exit does not list, or whose Exit is held on another node than its peers', has no internet there, and its status says why (from exits.rs's failed). Across nodes is not built, as for pods.

    With the exit down nothing leaves another way, at two layers. Routing: the Exit's table loses its route with its link, and rule 9012 answers unreachable; main's default route is never reached, 9010 suppressing it. The table: -out accepts the internet only out by kx<number>, whatever the routes say. Masquerade out by the tunnel takes its address (inferred, as T-0321 infers it). IPv6 to the internet is never carried for people (the exit carries IPv4 only): rules 9010 and 9012 and the table's IPv6 half refuse it.

    With an exit and the home's filter, the names leave by the filter's upstream, not the exit. A resolver address one of the node's own networks holds is asked there.

    How a configuration is shown once #

    POST /v1/peers (name, node, level) makes a key and applies the RemotePeer with its public half, as the caller, by the same path as any apply. It answers once with:

    [Interface]
PrivateKey = <shown this once>
Address = 100.89.23.7/32, fd8a:2b3c:4d5e::5e6f:7a8b:9c0d:1e2f/128
DNS = 10.1.0.53
[Peer]
PublicKey = <n1's people key, from its Node's status>
Endpoint = vpn.example.net:51821
AllowedIPs = 0.0.0.0/0, ::/0
PersistentKeepalive = 25

    AllowedIPs is everything for a level with internet (both families, so the phone's IPv6 does not go around the tunnel); otherwise the named ranges, the Services' /80 and the node's people addresses. It is what the phone sends, never what it may reach.

    The key is 32 bytes from ring's SystemRandom, as mesh.rs makes the node's. Its public half comes from the kernel, by a WireGuard link made for the one call, never up, then removed, as the mesh reads its own (mesh.rs, key_before_joining); no X25519 crate is in the tree. Asked again, a new key replaces the old one, whose configuration then stops working. The private key is in no store, history, audit line, log or file.

    The text: kith peer add NAME --node N --level L prints it, as kith node's recovery key is shown once (crates/kith-cli/src/main.rs): one call and a print, out of about 0.05 MB of room (2.93 of 3.00 MB, docs/numbers.json), measured in step 4. If it does not fit, the web UI alone shows configurations; no raise first.

    The QR code is drawn in the browser by a small encoder, crates/kithd/ui/qr.js (byte mode, one error-correction level) in ui.rs's FILES, on a canvas: the page's policy (script-src 'self', img-src 'self') refuses a data: image and allows this. A few KB of kithd's 1.65 MB of room (11.35 of 13.00 MB). The UI lists both kinds from /v1/schemas as any; new is "Add a peer" on the RemotePeer list: a dialog with the text, the QR and "whoever has this picture is this peer", forgotten when closed (no browser storage).

    Who may add a peer: an Identity whose Roles allow apply on RemotePeer with no namespace (access.rs, Grants::may); the route joins FOR_IDENTITIES, and the write is decided again where the log is kept, as every write is (SECURITY.md).

    A peer is not an Identity. Nothing maps a tunnel address to a Caller. With api: true a peer reaches the API's port, where TLS still asks for a client certificate; without one it is "anyone who reaches a node's port" (SECURITY.md).

    Approaches #

    1. People's devices as mesh peers, each an Identity. set_peers takes off every peer not a node (netlink.rs); on kith0 a phone sits beside nodes and Services, which trust mesh sources (services.rs, accept): one slip is a guest on the cluster. Refused.
    2. A proxy in kithd per level. TCP only, cut when kithd restarts, no LAN range, no UDP or ICMP, and a WireGuard stack in userspace as root. Refused.
    3. Services through the TLS ingress, certificates for people, no tunnel. No LAN range and nothing but HTTP, where the owner asked for the network. Part of an answer, for web apps.
    4. A device per level (kwg-guest, kwg-family), each on a port of its own. Rules by link, so T-0321's hold would serve as built. But the owner forwards a port per level, and a peer that changes level needs a new configuration. Not taken.
    5. One device for people, a table of its own, rules by source (chosen). The device and the table are writers that exist; each later step adds rules, not structure. Its cost: expressions written by hand, and a rule per peer.
    6. Allowed addresses alone, no table. They bound what a peer sends as, not where it goes. Refused.

    What a level does not hold #

    For SECURITY.md, said plainly:

    • DNS over HTTPS, a resolver on another port, a tunnel inside the tunnel: a level with internet can go around the filter.
    • Past the node a peer is the node: LAN hosts, routers and shares that trust addresses, and NetworkPolicies, see the node that ends its tunnel.
    • No limit on bandwidth.
    • A copied configuration is the peer until the peer is deleted. Nothing expires.
    • No permission per level: who may write one writes all.
    • The node that ends tunnels holds the people key and sees what is not TLS.
    • After kithd crashes, the last rules stand until it starts again.
    • kithd's proxied connection to a Service stays open, idle and passing nothing, up to an hour after a peer loses that Service.
    • IPv6 to the internet is not carried.
    • The phone decides what it sends into the tunnel; its other traffic is not the node's.
    • A name is not access: the resolver may answer names a peer cannot reach.

    Older nodes and roll-back #

    • A kithd without these kinds refuses them ("unknown kind": Resource::validated in lib.rs, Store::plan), and refuses them too as a replicated entry (crates/kith-store/src/machine.rs, apply). apply_all is all or nothing, so an older node's copy then lacks every resource of that apply. Apply them on their own, and only once every control node knows them, as the README says for trust.
    • A kithd without --people cannot end tunnels: given the flag it does not start (clap refuses it), and it ignores KITH_PEOPLE.
    • A worker is sent the RemotePeers that name it, their levels and those levels' Exits (view.rs, named and NAMES, as Exits are on T-0321's branch); its write list gains status.people on its Node and its peers' status.
    • An Exit with levels is refused, as an unknown field, by a node whose Exit lacks it.
    • Rolling back on kith OS restarts the machine: nothing listens, phones fail closed. On another OS, after a crash an older kithd leaves the last rules standing and unkept until a restart or the link is deleted by hand. The README says so.

    Built: slice A #

    AccessLevel and RemotePeer are kinds kith-api applies and checks (kinds!, Spec::check), and crates/kithd/src/people.rs gives each peer its two addresses on the leader and keeps them in its status, with the refusals of review condition 5. Not built, as the plan says: the device, the nftables table, --people, the configuration shown once, DNS and exits (slices B to D).

    Where the build differs from the text above:

    • An IPv6 overlap is not refused at apply: the mesh /48 and the people /64 come from the CA, which kith-api does not hold. kithd refuses an AccessLevel over either in its refused status instead. An IPv4 overlap with the people ranges or 100.88.0.0/16 is refused at apply.
    • The pods range checked is the default 100.88.0.0/16. The start-time check of the people ranges against --pod-range and the connected networks (condition 4) is not done yet.
    • An IPv4 address is 16 bits of the name's SHA-256 inside 100.89.0.0/16; the IPv6 address is the people /64 with the name's SHA-256 bytes 8 to 16 as its interface id. Of the peers that share a public key, one is given its addresses: the one already holding an address of ours, else the smallest name. The others are refused, naming it, and keep any address they held, so a refusal never frees one. A refusal names no address.
    • A refused peer keeps its addresses reserved in its status. Slice B, which routes or writes WireGuard config, uses a peer's addresses only when its refused is empty.

    Built: slice C #

    kithd --people [PORT] (env KITH_PEOPLE, default 51821; needs --mesh; root only) and --people-endpoint NAME:PORT (env KITH_PEOPLE_ENDPOINT) bring the people's device up: the key pki/people.key (0600, made once, kept across starts); the WireGuard device kwg0 with no peers, that key and the port, 100.89.0.1/16 and the people /64's ::1, up; the inet kith-people table written first, with its base chains and a masquerade for the people's IPv4 range. The node's status says people: {publicKey, port, endpoint} (publicKey base64, as a peer's). Without the flag, a start takes away kwg0, the table and the people status an earlier start left.

    Not built (slice D): the level chains, the node's networks, routing rules 9010/9012, and any peer on the device. The table is empty of those. The table is strict in production: a kernel with no masquerade (kith OS has CONFIG_NFT_MASQ=y) says so by name, and --people does not start. A test namespace on a host kernel with no masquerade (unshare -Urn here) makes the table without its masq rules, as kith-mesh's build_here does, and says so; the masq rule is covered by kith-mesh's expression tests.

    Built: slice D #

    A phone gets in: POST /v1/peers with {name, node, level} (people.rs, add; the route is in FOR_IDENTITIES, so the caller's own Roles decide). It makes a WireGuard key, applies a RemotePeer holding only the public half as the caller, and answers once, with Cache-Control: no-store, {name, node, level, publicKey, addresses, config}. The config is [Interface] (the private key, the peer's addresses, the level's DNS) and [Peer] (the node's people public key, the endpoint from --people-endpoint, the allowed ranges of the level). The refusals: 404 for no such node or level, 409 for a node whose status has no people (no --people-endpoint) or a level or address the plan refuses, 422 for a bad name. Asked again for the same name, it makes a new key and the old configuration stops working. A node as the caller is not told apart from an Identity.

    The node's own public key is read by kith_mesh::public_of, which asks the kernel for kwg0's key through netlink, as the mesh does; no key file is read for it.

    The private key is in a String in the handler and the answer, and in the CLI's or the page's variables while they show it. It is not in the store, the history, the audit line or kithd's log: the tests peers_route* and peers_config* look for it in each of them, and in the applied resource. It is not zeroed after use.

    kith peer add NAME --node N --level L prints the configuration and says it is shown once.

    The QR code is crates/kithd/ui/qr.js: ISO 18004 byte mode, error correction L, versions 1 to 40, drawn on a <canvas> (black on white, a four-module quiet zone) as the page's policy allows. It matches qrencode -8 -l L -m 0 on every length from 1 to 2953 bytes, tried once by hand; ui/test.mjs holds four of those outputs as fixtures. A configuration past the largest version shows its text with "No picture: too long for a QR code".

    The page: "Add a peer", on the RemotePeer list for a caller who may apply. A dialog asks the name, the node (only those with a people status) and the level; the answer opens a second dialog with the picture and the text, "Shown this once, and kept nowhere ... whoever has this picture is this peer". Closing the dialog wipes it; nothing goes to browser storage, and the test counts the key in each toast, question and call. ci/ui_shots.py takes the Peer and Qr views at 390 and 1440, light and dark, through a fetch shim, since the nodes it starts have no kwg0 (that needs root and a kernel WireGuard).

    Sizes, python3 ci/budgets.py on this lane's build: kith 2.96 MB (budget 3.00; docs/numbers.json had 2.92 before), kith-remote 3.59 (3.60), kithd 12.66 (13.00; numbers.json has 12). Those are the figures slice C's text above, 2.93 and 11.35 MB, should have named; the 11.35 was an estimate. kithd.order was written again (python3 ci/order.py), as the new handler's functions and slice C's were not in it.

    Open #

    • Should the filter tell people apart? Then the router routes 100.89.0.0/16 to the node and the node stops masquerading to the LAN; otherwise every peer is the node there.
    • A resolver hard-coded on 53: refused (here), or answered by the level's resolver (one nat rule)? Refused breaks the app; redirected filters it unseen.
    • Should a peer ever reach another peer (a laptop its phone)? Refused here.
    • Should a peer expire (a guest for a weekend)? Not here.

    Added by the review, each the owner's:

    • Who may make a peer of which level (access rules name kinds, not names). Until decided: a level that holds api or the internet needs the right to apply Levels as well.
    • Whether a level with the internet reaches the IPv6 LAN with no range named. Not until then.
    • What a --people node may be asked to forward to: the networks given with the flag.
